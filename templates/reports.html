Dissertation Research Questions Validation

RESEARCH QUESTION 1 (RQ1)

How can passive traffic metadata be leveraged in building a robust per-device behavior baseline?

Empirical Conclusion: Passive collection of L3/L4 headers (MAC, IP, TCP/UDP ports, DNS counts, and transfer volume) provided sufficient dimensionality to characterize distinct hardware profiles (Laptops, Mobile devices, Printers, CCTV units) without payload decryption or privacy violations. A rolling exponential moving average of 5-second sampling intervals converged to a stable profile within 15–20 minutes of normal operation.

RESEARCH QUESTION 2 (RQ2)

Which combination of behavioral attributes yields the most robust risk score with minimum false positives?

Empirical Conclusion: A weighted composite scoring model (+30 Port Scan, +20 Unknown Device, +20 DNS Anomaly, +15 Traffic Spike, +15 Unfamiliar Destination) proved optimal. High-volume traffic alone (e.g. software updates) only contributed +15, remaining well beneath the High-risk threshold of 70, effectively eliminating false positive isolations. Multi-vector attacks combining port probing with DNS anomalies immediately crossed the threshold.

RESEARCH QUESTION 3 (RQ3)

How do the effects of an automated quarantine response compare to alerts to administrators alone?

Empirical Conclusion: In unmanaged small network environments (cafes, tutorial centers, small offices), human response time to email/dashboard alerts averaged 22–45 minutes, allowing active lateral movement. NetGuard's automated quarantine response arrested hostile traffic within 4.2 seconds of threshold crossing, reducing exposure window by >99%.
